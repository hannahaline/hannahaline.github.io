Hard structures such as groins, seawalls, and jetties are built to hold the shoreline in place, but by trapping sand moving along the coast they also change the beaches around them. Most large-scale models of barrier island evolution leave them out, which limits what those models can say about the management choices communities actually face.

In this chapter, I am adding a groin module to the CASCADE model so that hard structures can be simulated alongside sea-level rise, storms, and the management strategies already in the model, such as beach nourishment and road relocation. The Buxton groin field on Hatteras Island is the test case: the satellite record shows how the shoreline on each side of the groins has changed over four decades.

### Approach

1. **Represent groins in CASCADE.** Add a groin module that changes how sand moves alongshore past a groin field, so hard structures can be simulated alongside nourishment, dune building, and road relocation.
2. **Test it at Buxton.** Compare the model with how the shoreline on each side of the Buxton groins has changed in the satellite record.
3. **Use it in forecasts.** Include hard structures in forecasts of Hatteras Island under different climate and management scenarios.

This chapter is in development; results will be shared here once they are published.
