<p class="hat-lead">
Hatteras Island today is partly a product of nature and partly a product of people.
For decades, people have built dunes, paved and moved a highway, pumped sand onto beaches, and built structures to hold the shore in place.
Each of these changes how sand moves, and therefore how the island changes.
</p>

{% include hatteras/timeline.liquid mode="management" %}

Open marks on the timeline are projects shown for context that the model does not include.
The model also leaves out the sand dredged from Oregon Inlet's navigation channel and placed on Pea Island beaches in several years (1990, 1999, 2000, 2003 and 2004), and the emergency fills on Pea Island after Hurricane Isabel (2003) and in 2013.

## Four kinds of management

<div class="hat-cards">
  <a class="hat-card" href="{{ '/hatteras/management/dunes/' | relative_url }}">
    <h3>Dune building</h3>
    <p>The line of dunes along most of the island was built by people in the 1930s and is rebuilt after storms. It keeps the road open, but also stops the overwash that builds the island up.</p>
  </a>
  <a class="hat-card" href="{{ '/hatteras/management/nc-12/' | relative_url }}">
    <h3>NC-12</h3>
    <p>The island's only road. Where the ocean came too close, it was moved inland in 1989 and 1999, and onto bridges after 2011.</p>
  </a>
  <a class="hat-card" href="{{ '/hatteras/management/nourishment/' | relative_url }}">
    <h3>Beach nourishment</h3>
    <p>Four projects since 2014 pumped sand onto the beach at Rodanthe, Buxton and Avon. See how far each one moved the shoreline, and how long it lasted.</p>
  </a>
  <a class="hat-card" href="{{ '/hatteras/management/groins/' | relative_url }}">
    <h3>Groins</h3>
    <p>Three walls built across the beach at Buxton in 1969–70, photographed from 1993 to 2024 with the shoreline each year.</p>
  </a>
</div>

These actions work together. The dunes protect the road, the road decides where dunes are kept, and nourishment and groins hold the beach in front of both.
The model includes all four, so we can ask how the island would have changed without them.
