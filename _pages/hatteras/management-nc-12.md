NC-12 is the only road on and off Hatteras Island. Where it runs close to the ocean, every big storm threatens to wash sand over it or cut through it. Keeping it open shapes how the rest of the beach is managed.
## How the road came to Hatteras

Until the early 1950s there was no paved road down Hatteras Island. People reached the mainland by boat, and a car trip meant some 60 miles up the beach and a ferry across Oregon Inlet, letting tyres down and digging out of the sand along the way.

- **1936–40.** The National Park Service had crews build a continuous line of dunes along the Outer Banks, in part to make room for a road behind it (see [dune building]({{ '/hatteras/management/dunes/' | relative_url }})).
- **1937.** Congress authorised Cape Hatteras National Seashore. The villages that were already built up were left out of it, and local support was won partly with the promise of a road.
- **1945.** The Cape Hatteras Electric Cooperative was founded; before that, only Hatteras Village had electric service.
- **1952.** A road was completed through Pea Island to Hatteras village.
- **1953.** The Park Service took over the seashore, which was dedicated in 1958.
- **1963.** The Bonner Bridge opened across Oregon Inlet, the first bridge onto Hatteras Island; before it, cars came over on a ferry.

The seashore, the bridge and the road opened up this stretch of coast. In 1950 about 1,600 people lived in the villages of Hatteras Island. Today more than two million people visit the seashore each year: about 1.5 million in 1980 and 2.6 million in 2020.
Houses, motels and the road went up right behind the built dunes, and keeping that line open is now a large, ongoing job: since 2010 NCDOT has spent more than $72 million keeping NC-12 passable south of Oregon Inlet after storms.

## Moving the road

Where the ocean has come too close, the road has been moved:

- **1989, the S-curves north of Rodanthe.** The road was rebuilt farther inland, by up to about 110 m.
- **1999, north of Buxton.** After Hurricane Dennis, a stretch of road was moved inland by up to about 75 m.
- **2011 and 2017, New Inlet on Pea Island.** Hurricane Irene cut through the island, and the road now crosses the {% include hatteras/term.liquid id="breach" text="breach" %} on a bridge.
- **2022, the Jug Handle Bridge.** The road at the S-curves now leaves the island and crosses Pamlico Sound, so that stretch of beach no longer has a road behind it.
